Think like an investor

Set the interest rate to 5%. Then move the maturity date several years into the future. What happens to the bond price? Why?

At a positive, unchanged annual yield, a longer time to maturity lowers the present value of the promised future payment. Investors must wait longer to receive their money.

Assumptions: full repayment, annual compounding, no transaction costs, nonnegative yields (0–25%), and Actual/365 Fixed day count. Time to maturity equals remaining calendar days divided by 365. Changing face value or maturity holds annual yield constant. The default maturity is 365 days from today, making the initial investment exactly one year under this convention.
